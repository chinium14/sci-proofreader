# Effect of Caffeine Supplementation on Reaction Time in Sleep-Deprived Adults

## Abstract

Sleep deprivation impair cognitive performance, and caffeine is widely used as a countermeasure. We investigated the effect of caffeine on reaction time in 60 sleep-deprived adults. Participants who received caffeine responded significantly faster than those who received placebo (mean reaction time 312 ms vs 356 ms). These results prove that caffeine fully restores cognitive function after sleep loss.

## 1. Introduction

It is well known that sleep loss slows reaction time. Caffeine is an adenosine receptor antagonist, and it's stimulant effects have been studied for a long time. However, many studies have reported that caffeine improves vigilance during extended wakefulness [1, 2]. Despite of this, little is known about the dose-response relationship in adults deprived of sleep for more than 24 hours.

The aim of this study was twofold: (i) to test whether a single dose of caffeine improves reaction time after 30 h of wakefulness, and (ii) to determine whether the effect differs between habitual and non-habitual caffeine consumers.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Participants

Sixty-four healthy adults (aged 20–35 years) were recruited from the university community. Subjects were randomly assigned to a caffeine group (n = 32) or a placebo group (n = 30). All volunteers gave written informed consent.

### 2.2 Procedure

Participants stayed awake for 30 h under supervision. After that, they received either a capsule with 200 mg caffeine or a placebo capsule, and it was tested with the psychomotor vigilance task (PVT) some time later. Using a standard laptop, the reaction time of each participant were recorded over 10 minutes. Data was analysed with a t-test and p < 0.05 was considered significant.

## 3. Results

Mean reaction time in the caffeine group was 312 ± 41 ms, compared to 365 ± 48 ms in the placebo group (Table 1). The difference of 44 ms was significant (p = 0.07). The number of lapses were also much lower in the caffeine group. Interestingly, the caffeine group showed a really huge improvement in subjective alertness, which was amazing given the small dose.

| Group | n | Mean RT (ms) | SD |
|-------|---|--------------|----|
| Caffeine | 32 | 312 | 41 |
| Placebo | 30 | 365 | 48 |

Reaction time was negatively correlated with habitual coffee intake across all participants (r = −0.21). Therefore, habitual caffeine consumption causes faster reactions under sleep deprivation.

## 4. Discussion

Our findings show that caffeine improves reaction time after sleep deprivation. Because the caffeine group was faster, caffeine must act by blocking adenosine A2A receptors in the striatum. The dopamine levels measured in our participants supports this mechanism. Since the effect was observed in young university volunteers, it will apply to shift workers, older adults and patients with insomnia.

A limitation of this study is that plasma caffeine concentrations was not measured, so the dose reaching the brain is unknown. In conclusion, caffeine is the best countermeasure for sleep loss and should be recommended always.
